## 📊 Using MiniWatts

### Main Dashboard

When you open the app, you'll see a clean dashboard with several reading displays. Don't worry if it looks complex — each number is explained below.

### Understanding the Readings

- **Charger Watts** - This shows the actual power coming from your wall charger. If your charger is rated for 20W, you might only see 18W here due to cable losses.
- **Cell Watts** - This shows the real power reaching your battery cell. It's always slightly less than the charger watts because of heating and internal resistance.
- **Temperature Sensors** - You'll see a list of all 30 temperature sensors in your iPhone. They're named by location, like "Battery", "Logic Board", "Camera", etc.
- **PD Handshake** - This shows the USB Power Delivery negotiation. You'll see the voltage and current your charger is providing, plus the charging protocol name.

### Taking Measurements

To get the most accurate readings:

1. **Use a good cable** - Cheap cables can limit charging speed
2. **Don't use your phone** - Heavy usage while charging affects the numbers
3. **Wait 2-3 minutes** - Readings stabilize after a few minutes of connection
4. **Try different chargers** - Compare your Apple charger vs. third-party ones

## 🔬 Real-World Scenarios

### Scenario 1: "Is my fast charger actually fast?"

Plug in your 20W charger and check the Charger Watts reading. If you see around 18-19W, it's working correctly. If you only see 5W, your cable might be bad or your phone is too hot.

### Scenario 2: "Is my battery degrading?"

Check the battery temperature while charging. Healthy batteries stay under 95°F (35°C). If you see temperatures above 110°F (43°C), your battery may need service.

### Scenario 3: "Which charger is best?"

Test all your chargers and note the Charger Watts. The one showing the highest watts is your best charger — but also check the Cell Watts. Sometimes a "faster" charger wastes more energy as heat.

## ❓ Troubleshooting

### Problem: MiniWatts won't open

**Solution**: Make sure you've trusted the developer in Settings → General → Device Management. Also, check that your iPhone has iOS 14 or newer.

### Problem: Readings show zero

**Solution**: Check your USB cable. Try a different cable or port. Also, make sure your phone is actually charging — some cables only transfer data, not power.

### Problem: Some temperatures are not shown

**Solution**: Older iPhone models have fewer temperature sensors. If you have an iPhone 11 or newer, you should see all 30 sensors. Older models show fewer.

### Problem: Charger watts seem low

**Solution**: Measure again after 3 minutes of charging. The first few minutes are "testing" time where the charger negotiates the power level. Also, check that your phone is below 80% battery — charging slows down above that.
